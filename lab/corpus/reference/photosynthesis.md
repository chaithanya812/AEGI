## Definition {page=1}

Photosynthesis is the process by which green plants, algae and some bacteria convert light
energy into chemical energy stored as carbohydrates. Carbon dioxide and water are consumed
and oxygen is released as a by-product.

The overall balanced equation is six molecules of carbon dioxide plus six molecules of
water, driven by light energy, yielding one molecule of glucose and six molecules of
oxygen.

## Location and pigments {page=1}

In plants the process takes place in the chloroplasts, organelles containing the green
pigment chlorophyll. Chlorophyll absorbs light most strongly in the blue and red parts of
the visible spectrum and reflects green light, which is why foliage appears green.

## The two stages {page=2}

The light-dependent reactions occur in the thylakoid membranes. Light energy splits water,
releasing oxygen and generating ATP and NADPH.

The light-independent reactions, known as the Calvin cycle, occur in the stroma. They use
the ATP and NADPH produced in the first stage to fix carbon dioxide into three-carbon
sugars, which are assembled into glucose.

## Significance {page=2}

Photosynthesis is the primary route by which energy enters most food webs, and it is the
origin of the oxygen in Earth's atmosphere.
