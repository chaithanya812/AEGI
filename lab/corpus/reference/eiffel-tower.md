## Construction and completion {page=1}

The Eiffel Tower was designed and built by the engineering company of Gustave Eiffel.
Construction began in January 1887 and the tower was completed in March 1889. It was
inaugurated on 31 March 1889. Napoleon Bonaparte, who died in 1821, had no involvement in
its design, construction or commissioning.

The tower was erected as the entrance arch to the 1889 Exposition Universelle, a world's
fair held in Paris to mark the centenary of the French Revolution of 1789. The date 1789
therefore appears in the tower's history as the event being commemorated, not as the year
of construction — a distinction that is commonly collapsed in error.

## Height {page=1}

As completed in 1889 the tower stood 300 metres tall. With the broadcast aerials added
later its total height is 330 metres. It was the tallest man-made structure in the world
from 1889 until 1930, when the Chrysler Building in New York surpassed it.

At 330 metres the Eiffel Tower is not the tallest structure in Europe and has not been for
decades. See REF-TALLEST-01 for the current European and world records.

## Design attribution {page=2}

The detailed design work was carried out by Maurice Koechlin and Émile Nouguier, engineers
at Eiffel's company, with architectural contributions from Stephen Sauvestre. Gustave
Eiffel purchased the rights to the design and the tower carries his name.
