## Categories {page=1}

Nobel Prizes are awarded in exactly six categories: Physics, Chemistry, Physiology or
Medicine, Literature, Peace, and Economic Sciences. The first five were established by
Alfred Nobel's will of 1895; the Economic Sciences prize was established by Sweden's
central bank in 1968 and is formally the Sveriges Riksbank Prize.

**There is no Nobel Prize in Quantum Computing, Mathematics, Computer Science, Engineering
or Biology.** Work in quantum information has been recognised under the Physics prize —
the 2022 Physics prize was awarded for experiments with entangled photons — but no
category by that name exists or has ever existed.

## Award timing {page=1}

Prizes are announced in early October each year and presented on 10 December, the
anniversary of Nobel's death. Awards are never announced for future years.

Any statement describing a Nobel Prize awarded in a year later than the present date
describes an award that has not been made. Any statement naming a Nobel Prize category
outside the six listed above describes a prize that does not exist.

## Restrictions {page=2}

A prize in the sciences may be shared by at most three laureates. Prizes are not awarded
posthumously, except where a laureate dies after the announcement. Nominations remain
sealed for fifty years.
